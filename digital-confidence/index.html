Eight-week community learning program

Digital Confidence for the AI Era

A practical learning journey for adults who want to feel more capable with technology—beginning with everyday digital foundations and building toward informed, responsible AI use.

The purpose

Help people understand enough to participate, decide, and act for themselves.

Format
One guided session each week
Starting level
No technical background assumed
Approach
Explain, practise, apply, repeat
Setting
Adaptable for community delivery

Why this matters

Access to technology is not the same as confidence using it.

Many essential tasks now assume that people can manage accounts, files, online forms, cloud services, security decisions, and constant software changes. Instructions often explain where to click without helping people understand what is happening.

AI adds another layer. People are being encouraged to use it before they have been given a clear explanation of what it does, where it fails, what information should stay private, or when human judgment matters.

This program closes that gap gradually. It starts with the digital foundations people need now and introduces AI only after confidence, safety, and context are in place.

Who it is for

Adults who want a patient, practical route into technology

The program is designed around common barriers, not labels. People can arrive with different devices, experiences, and goals.

Learning principles

Confidence is built through understanding and practice.

Plain language

New terms are explained in context instead of being used as shortcuts.

Hands-on learning

Participants practise useful tasks during each session, not sometime later.

Safety before speed

Privacy, scams, passwords, and informed choices are part of the foundation.

Transferable understanding

The goal is not one app. Learners build knowledge they can carry to new tools.

The learning journey

Eight weeks, from foundations to practical AI

Each week adds one manageable layer. Examples and activities can be adapted to the needs of the participating community.

  1. Week 01

    Starting point and technology foundations

    Identify current confidence, personal goals, device basics, common terminology, and how digital systems connect.

  2. Week 02

    Files, cloud storage, and digital organization

    Understand where files live, create a simple folder structure, recognize syncing, and find information again.

  3. Week 03

    Online safety, scams, and account security

    Build stronger passwords, use multi-factor authentication, recognize warning signs, and respond when something goes wrong.

  4. Week 04

    Everyday productivity and digital communication

    Work with email, documents, calendars, links, attachments, and online meetings more confidently.

  5. Week 05

    AI foundations

    Learn what generative AI is, how it produces answers, where it appears, what it can do, and why it can be wrong.

  6. Week 06

    Practical AI for everyday life

    Use AI for explanation, planning, brainstorming, and drafting while protecting personal information and checking outputs.

  7. Week 07

    AI for employment, learning, and opportunity

    Practise responsible ways to research roles, prepare questions, understand documents, and support—not replace—personal work.

  8. Week 08

    Your personal digital toolkit

    Review progress, choose reliable tools and support resources, make a safety plan, and set realistic next learning steps.

How it can be delivered

Structured enough to guide; flexible enough to meet people where they are.

Weekly facilitated sessions

One focused theme, a clear demonstration, supported practice, and time for questions.

Optional support time

Additional help gives learners space to repeat a task without holding back the group.

Community-specific examples

Exercises can reflect the services, devices, languages, and priorities participants actually encounter.

Take-away resources

Plain-language guides reinforce the learning after each session and support continued independence.

Practical outcomes

What participants should be able to do

The objective is not technical expertise. It is increased independence, safer choices, and the confidence to keep learning.

  • Organize and find their own files with less frustration
  • Explain basic cloud storage and account concepts in their own words
  • Use stronger account-security habits and recognize common scams
  • Complete everyday digital communication and productivity tasks
  • Describe what generative AI can and cannot reliably do
  • Use AI for a practical task while protecting privacy and checking the result
  • Know where to find trusted help and what question to ask next
