THE BIG PICTURE

What Happens When You Click "Pay Now"?

It might feel like your credit card information goes directly from your computer to the online store. In reality, that's only part of the story.

Behind every online purchase is a network of businesses working together to process your payment securely. Your information is encrypted, verified, checked for fraud, approved by your bank, and sent back to the retailer— all in just a few seconds.

Understanding this process helps explain why online payments are generally much safer today than many people realize.

STEP 1

Your Information Is Encrypted

The moment you enter your credit card details, your browser begins protecting that information using a technology called encryption.

Encryption converts readable information into coded data that can only be understood by the intended recipient. If someone intercepted the data while it was travelling across the internet, it would appear as meaningless characters rather than your actual card number.

Think of it like this

Imagine sending a letter written in a secret code that only the recipient knows how to read. Anyone who intercepted the letter would see the message, but they wouldn't understand what it says.

Encryption works in much the same way.

STEP 2

Your Payment Travels Over a Secure Connection

Before your payment information leaves your device, your browser checks whether the website is using a secure connection.

Websites that begin with https:// use encryption to help protect information as it travels between your device and the website.

You may also notice a padlock icon beside the website address.

Did You Know?

The padlock means your connection is encrypted—it does not guarantee the website itself is trustworthy. Scam websites can also use HTTPS, so it's still important to verify you're shopping with a reputable business.
