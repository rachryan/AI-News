SECURITY FUNDAMENTALS

How Your Credit Card Is Protected Online

Every time you shop online, your payment travels through several security checks in just a few seconds. Understanding what happens behind the scenes can help you shop with confidence and recognize how businesses work together to protect your payment information.

THE BIG PICTURE

What Happens When You Click "Pay Now"?

Many people imagine their credit card information goes directly from their computer to the online store. In reality, several different companies work together to process your payment securely.

Your information is encrypted. It travels across a secure connection. A payment processor receives it. Your bank checks the transaction. The retailer receives an approval or decline.

All of this usually happens in just a few seconds.

Your payment journey

🛒 You → 🌐 Website → 💳 Payment Processor → 🏦 Your Bank → ✅ Approval

STEP 1

Your Information Is Encrypted

The moment you enter your credit card details, your browser begins protecting that information using a technology called encryption.

Encryption converts readable information into coded data that can only be read by the intended recipient. If someone intercepted the data while it travelled across the internet, they wouldn't see your actual credit card number—they would only see unreadable data.

💡 Think of it like this

Imagine writing a letter in a secret code that only the recipient knows how to read. Anyone who intercepted the letter could see it, but they wouldn't understand what it says. Encryption works much the same way.

STEP 2

Your Payment Travels Over a Secure Connection

Before your payment leaves your device, your browser checks that the website is using a secure connection.

Websites beginning with https:// use encryption to protect the information travelling between your device and the website.

Most browsers also display a padlock beside the website address.

💡 Did You Know?

The padlock means your connection is encrypted. It does not guarantee that the website itself is trustworthy. Fake websites can also use HTTPS, so always make sure you're shopping with a business you recognize.
