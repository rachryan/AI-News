What to Do If You've Been Hacked

Discovering that one of your online accounts has been hacked can feel overwhelming, but it doesn't have to turn into a disaster. Acting quickly and following a few simple steps can help you regain control and limit the damage.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

In this guide

  • How to recognize the signs of a compromised account
  • The first steps you should take
  • How to secure your accounts
  • When to contact your bank or service provider
  • How to reduce the chances of it happening again
FIRST THINGS FIRST

Don't Panic—Take Action

It's normal to feel anxious if you think you've been hacked. Fortunately, many security incidents can be resolved if you act quickly.

Your goal is to secure your accounts before anyone can do additional harm.

💡 Think of it like this

If someone stole the keys to your home, the first thing you'd do is change the locks—not spend hours wondering how it happened.

Online accounts work much the same way. Focus on securing your accounts first, then investigate what happened.

STEP 1

Change Your Password Immediately

If you can still access your account, change your password right away.

Choose a strong, unique password that you haven't used anywhere else. If you use the same password on other accounts, change those as well.

STEP 2

Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

If MFA isn't already enabled, turn it on immediately after changing your password.

This adds another layer of protection and makes it much harder for someone to regain access.

STEP 3

Check for Unauthorized Activity

Review your recent account activity carefully.

  • Unexpected purchases
  • Password reset emails you didn't request
  • Unknown devices signed into your account
  • Messages or emails you didn't send
  • Changes to your recovery email or phone number
STEP 4

Contact the Organization

If the account contains financial information or sensitive personal data, contact the company as soon as possible.

Banks, credit card companies, and many online services have dedicated fraud or account recovery teams that can help secure your account.

STEP 5

Watch Your Other Accounts

If one account was compromised, there's a chance the attacker may try to access others using the same email address or password.

Monitor your email, banking, shopping, and social media accounts over the next several days for unusual activity.

TECHTIDY TIP

Act Quickly, Don't Panic

Most people lose more time worrying than actually securing their accounts. A calm, methodical response is often the most effective one.

🛡️ Remember This

Act quickly, but don't panic.

Secure your accounts first, then investigate what happened. Quick action often prevents a small problem from becoming a much larger one.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to Remember

  • Change your password immediately.
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication.
  • Review your account for suspicious activity.
  • Contact the organization if financial or personal information is involved.
  • Monitor your other accounts and update reused passwords.
