DIGITAL PRODUCTIVITY · LESSON 4

Build a Simple Digital Workflow

A workflow is simply the repeatable route work follows from arrival to completion. Making that route visible often helps more than adding another app.

Beginner · About 14 minutes · Includes an activity and knowledge check

What You’ll Learn

  • How to map capture, decision, action and review
  • How to find duplication and unclear ownership
  • How to choose the simplest suitable tool
  • When automation or AI should—and should not—be added

Make the Work Visible First

Begin with the current process, including awkward manual steps. Identify where work arrives, who decides what happens, where the action occurs and how completion is confirmed.

Improve before automating

Automation makes a clear process faster. It can also make a confused process fail faster. Remove unnecessary steps and clarify ownership first.

Choose Tools After the Process

DOCUMENTBest for guidance, narrative information or a reusable template.
SPREADSHEETUseful for a modest table, calculations and simple tracking.
FORM + DATABASEBetter for consistent intake, many records, permissions and status changes.
AUTOMATIONUseful for stable, repeated rules with clear exceptions and an accountable owner.

Use AI only where the information and consequence allow it

AI may help draft or classify low-risk material, but do not send confidential information to an unapproved tool or let AI make high-stakes final decisions.

Try It: Map a Fictional Request

A community group receives workshop requests by email. Map four steps and name one improvement.

Show one sensible answer

Capture: copy approved request details into one tracker. Decide: coordinator checks eligibility and capacity. Act: schedule or decline. Review: send confirmation and record status. Improvement: use a simple approved form to reduce missing information.

Quick Knowledge Check

What comes before automation?

Answer

Understanding and simplifying the actual process.

What makes a workflow trustworthy?

Answer

Clear inputs, decisions, ownership, permissions, exceptions and confirmation of completion.

What to Remember

  • Map the real process before choosing tools.
  • Clarify who decides and who acts.
  • Remove duplication before automating.
  • Use the simplest tool that safely meets the need.
  • Review whether the workflow still works.
