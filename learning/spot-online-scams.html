How to Spot Online Scams

Online scams are becoming more convincing every year. Learning to recognize the warning signs can help you protect your money, your personal information, and your online accounts.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

In this guide

  • How scammers try to trick people
  • The most common warning signs

  • What to do if something feels suspicious
  • Simple habits that reduce your risk
THE BIG PICTURE

Why Online Scams Work

Most scams don't rely on hacking computers—they rely on tricking people.

Scammers often create a sense of urgency, fear, curiosity, or excitement to encourage quick decisions before you have time to think.

💡 Think of it like this

A scam is like someone pretending to be a trusted friend so you'll unlock the door yourself.

The goal isn't to break in—they want you to let them in.

WARNING SIGNS

Red Flags to Watch For

Many scams share the same warning signs.

  • Messages that create urgency.
  • Requests for passwords or verification codes.
  • Unexpected attachments or links.
  • Offers that seem too good to be true.
  • Poor spelling or unusual wording.
PHISHING

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trusted organization to trick you into revealing personal information.

These messages may appear to come from your bank, a delivery company, a streaming service, or even someone you know.

Before clicking a link, take a moment to check who actually sent the message.

PAUSE BEFORE YOU CLICK

Five Questions to Ask Yourself

  • Was I expecting this message?
  • Is the sender who they claim to be?
  • Does the link look legitimate?
  • Am I being pressured to act quickly?
  • Can I verify this another way?
IF YOU'RE UNSURE

What Should You Do?

If something feels suspicious, trust your instincts.

  • Don't click links immediately.
  • Visit the company's website directly.
  • Call the organization using a trusted phone number.
  • Delete suspicious emails or messages.
  • Ask someone you trust if you're unsure.
TECHTIDY TIP

When in Doubt, Slow Down

Most online scams succeed because they convince people to act quickly. They create urgency, fear, excitement, or curiosity so you don't stop to think.

Before clicking a link, downloading an attachment, or sharing personal information, pause for a moment and ask yourself whether the request makes sense.

🛡️ Remember This

When in doubt, slow down.

A legitimate company will almost always give you time to verify a request. Scammers don't want you to stop and think.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to Remember

  • Scammers target people—not just technology.
  • Urgency is one of the biggest warning signs.
  • Never share passwords or MFA codes.
  • Verify unexpected requests before responding.
  • When in doubt, slow down.
