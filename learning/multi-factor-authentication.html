Multi-Factor Authentication Explained

Even the strongest password can be stolen. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) adds an extra layer of protection by requiring a second step before someone can access your account.

THE BASICS

What Is Multi-Factor Authentication?

Multi-Factor Authentication (often shortened to MFA) requires you to prove your identity using two or more methods before you can sign in.

Instead of relying only on your password, MFA asks for an additional verification step. Even if someone knows your password, they usually can't access your account without that second factor.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why Isn't a Password Enough?

Passwords can be guessed, stolen in data breaches, or tricked out of people through phishing scams.

If someone discovers your password, they may be able to sign in as if they were you.

MFA makes that much harder because the attacker also needs access to something you physically have or something unique to you.

THE THREE FACTORS

The Three Ways You Can Verify Your Identity

Most authentication methods fall into one of these three categories:

  • Something you know – your password or PIN.
  • Something you have – your phone, security key, or authenticator app.
  • Something you are – your fingerprint, face, or other biometric information.

💡 Think of it like this

Imagine your house has two locks.

Even if someone copies your front door key, they still can't get inside without the code to the second lock.

MFA works the same way by adding another barrier between attackers and your account.

COMMON METHODS

How MFA Works in Everyday Life

Many websites and apps use MFA in one of these ways:

  • A code sent by text message.
  • A code generated by an authenticator app.
  • A prompt asking you to approve the sign-in on your phone.
  • A fingerprint or Face ID check.
  • A physical security key inserted into your device.
BEST PRACTICE

Which Type Is Best?

Any form of MFA is better than using only a password.

However, security experts generally recommend using an authenticator app or a physical security key instead of text messages whenever possible.

Text messages can still be intercepted in rare situations, while authenticator apps generate codes directly on your device.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to Remember

  • Passwords alone are not always enough.
  • MFA adds a second layer of protection.
  • Enable MFA on email, banking, shopping, and social media accounts.
  • Authenticator apps are generally more secure than text messages.
  • The small extra step greatly improves your online security.
