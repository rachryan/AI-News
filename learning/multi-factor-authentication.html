Multi-Factor Authentication Explained

Passwords are important, but they're only one part of protecting your online accounts. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) adds a second layer of security, making it much harder for someone else to sign in—even if they know your password.

WHAT IS MFA?

A Second Layer of Protection

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) asks you to verify your identity using two or more methods before you can sign in.

Even if someone steals your password, they usually can't access your account without the second verification step.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why Passwords Aren't Always Enough

Passwords can be guessed, stolen in data breaches, or obtained through phishing scams.

MFA helps stop attackers because knowing your password isn't enough—they also need access to your phone, security key, or biometric information.

THE THREE FACTORS

The Three Ways You Can Verify Your Identity

  • 🔑 Something you know
    Your password or PIN.
  • 📱 Something you have
    Your phone, authenticator app, or security key.
  • 👤 Something you are
    Your fingerprint or Face ID.

💡 Think of it like this

Imagine your front door has two locks instead of one. Even if someone copies your key, they still can't get inside without unlocking the second lock.

That's exactly what MFA does for your online accounts.

COMMON METHODS

How MFA Works

Depending on the website or app, your second verification might be:

  • A code sent by text message.
  • A code generated by an authenticator app.
  • A notification asking you to approve the sign-in.
  • A fingerprint or Face ID.
  • A physical security key.
WHERE TO USE MFA

Which Accounts Should You Protect?

If you can only enable MFA on a few accounts, start with the ones that matter most.

  • 📧 Email
  • 🏦 Banking
  • 🔐 Password Manager
  • 🛒 Shopping Accounts
  • 📱 Social Media
BEST PRACTICE

Which MFA Method Is Best?

Any form of MFA is better than using only a password.

If you have a choice, security experts generally recommend using an authenticator app or a physical security key instead of text messages.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to Remember

  • Passwords alone aren't enough.
  • MFA adds a second layer of protection.
  • Turn on MFA for your email first.
  • Authenticator apps are generally the best everyday option.
  • A few extra seconds during sign-in can greatly improve your security.
